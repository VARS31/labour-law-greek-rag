# Evaluation report: measuring the one-shot baseline (Phase 2)

How good is the one-shot setup from Phase 1C, measured on a fixed set of questions with written-down answers? This file is generated by [`scripts/eval_report.py`](../scripts/eval_report.py) from the stored runs in [`eval/results/`](../eval/results/). Don't edit it by hand.

This report measures the setup of Phase 2, with equal-weight RRF. Phase 3A has since changed the default way of combining search results and the refusal rule: see [retrieval-report.md](retrieval-report.md). Phase 3B has changed the default embedding and the repeat filter: see [enhancer-report.md](enhancer-report.md).

**The baseline is far from good enough, and now we know by how much.** On the test split the right chunk reached the model for 12 of 17 questions, and 7 of 16 answerable questions got every key fact. The model refused 2 of the 4 questions it should have refused, and wrongly refused 2 it could have answered.

- **Search and reading fail about equally often.** Over dev and test together, 18 answers fell short because the right chunk never arrived and 12 because the model misused what it had, or answered when it should have refused.
- **Search usually finds the chunk but doesn't deliver it.** On dev the right chunk is in the top 50 for 24 of 27 questions, but among the 8 for only 13.
- **Hybrid search, as built, is the weak link.** Semantic search alone has the right chunk in its top 8 for 20 of 27 dev questions. Keyword search has it in its top 50 for only 15, and fusion then pushes down much of what semantic search found. This number isn't a fair race yet, because semantic's top 8 still contains repeats, but the gap is large. Phase 1B had already seen a sign of this.
- **Vocabulary mismatches fail almost completely on dev.** None of the 4 got the right chunk among the 8.
- **The citation check doesn't tell good answers from bad.** On dev, 4 of the 11 answers that passed it had every key fact, against 5 of the 15 that failed. It catches altered quotes, which is useful, but it's not a measure of quality.

**Test looks better than dev, and that isn't an improvement.** Dev holds the harder questions: all 9 that are close to the Phase 1C questions were kept there, along with 4 of the 6 vocabulary items. With 20 questions, the test numbers also move a lot on a single answer.

**Time** is about 30 to 60 seconds per question, nearly all of it the model writing. Search takes well under a second.

## What the test set contains

[`eval/questions.jsonl`](../eval/questions.jsonl) has 52 questions in everyday modern Greek. Each has 2 to 4 key facts a correct answer must contain, its gold sources (law, article, chunk) and notes on its traps. I wrote every item by reading the chunks, not from general knowledge. Where our data differs from today's law, the expected answer follows our data.

42 questions can be answered from our data and 10 can't. Those 10 are the 8 that must be refused and 2 of the follow-a-reference items, whose law is missing: for them the right answer says it wasn't found.

**Dev and test.** Dev is for looking at and improving. Test is for one final measurement per system, and the code refuses a second run of the same system. Questions close to the eight from Phase 1C, on which the prompt was revised, were kept in dev.

**Who checked it.** Code confirmed that every gold quote is in its chunk and that no question copies the law's wording. The project owner reviewed 20 items, the ones where my reading could most easily be wrong: 19 kept, 1 edited ([`eval/review.md`](../eval/review.md)). The other 32 had no second reader, so some expected answers may still be wrong.

## How it is measured

`.venv/bin/python -m labour_rag.evaluate --split dev` runs every question and saves the results in `eval/results/<date>_<config>/`, so runs can be compared.

| Measure | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Recall@8 | Was a correct chunk among the 8 given to the model? |
| Recall@50 | Was it in the top 50 of hybrid search? If yes but not in the 8, a better way of choosing could reach it. |
| MRR | How high it ranked: 1.00 means always first, 0.50 always second, 0 outside the top 50. |
| Key facts | The share of a question's key facts found in the answer, averaged over the answerable questions. |
| Every key fact | Answers that contain all their facts. Closest to "the answer is complete". |
| Gold law cited | Did the answer cite a chunk from a law it should use? |
| Citation check passed | Every [n] exists and every quote is in its chunk. It says nothing about whether the reasoning is right. |
| Correct refusal | A question our data can't answer got an answer saying it isn't in the sources. |
| Wrong refusal | A question our data can answer got an answer saying it isn't in the sources. |
| Search problem | The answer fell short and the right chunk wasn't among the 8. |
| Reading problem | The answer fell short although the right chunk was among the 8, or the model answered a question it should have refused. |

**How far to trust the key-fact score.** Matching needs no model. A fact counts as found if all its numbers are in the answer and enough of its other words are: half if it has a number, 60% if not. It ignores accents and word endings, and it treats "τρεις μήνες", "3 μήνες" and "τρίμηνο" as the same. I read all 32 baseline answers and marked all 71 facts of the answerable ones myself. The matching agrees on **68 of 71**. I tuned it once on these dev answers, before the test run, and didn't change it after. It still can't understand meaning: it can't see "δεν", and a number used in another sentence still counts. The disagreements that remain:

- `lookup-03`: "Με απόφαση του Υπουργού Εργασίας, κάθε χρόνο", which the matching marked as found.
- `sector-01`: "Ισχύει για εργάτες και τεχνίτες με πάνω από πέντε χρόνια", which the matching marked as found.
- `single-10`: "Τρεις μήνες από τη λύση της σχέσης εργασίας για άκυρη απόλυση", which the matching marked as not found.

**Refusals** count if the answer says near its start that the answer isn't in the sources, as "Δεν βρέθηκε" or in other words: `lookup-01` wrote «δεν αναφέρεται στα αποσπάσματα». Until Phase 3A only the exact words counted, and [retrieval-report.md](retrieval-report.md) shows what that changed. The check doesn't ask whether a refusal names where the rule would be, which `reference-01` and `reference-03` expect. Neither was refused here, so no number changes, but it could matter in Phase 4.

**The sample is small.** On dev, one question moves recall by about 4 points and the share of complete answers by about 4. Small differences below are noise.

**Limit 4 gets the right chunk to the model more often, but the answers don't improve.** Recall@8 rises by 3 questions (`lookup-01`, `reference-02`, `single-21`). **On its own, qwen3 looks better. Through hybrid search, almost none of that reaches the model.** Semantic search alone has the right chunk in its top 8 for 23 of 27 questions with qwen3, against 20 of 27 with bge-m3. It gains 4 (`sector-02`, `single-10`, `single-29`, `vocab-03`) and loses 1 (`reference-01`, which falls from rank 1 to rank 27 with the instruction and to rank 14 without it). But among the 8 chunks the model really gets, the count only goes from 13 to 14, and 10 of those 23 never arrive. This supports the main finding of this report: the limit is how the two lists are combined, and a better embedding model can't make up for that.

**The evidence is thin.** Four gains against one loss would happen by pure chance about one time in three. The small gain in hybrid search also hides more movement: with the instruction 3 questions come in and 2 drop out, without it 2 come in and 1 drops out. No answers were written with qwen3, so we don't know whether answers would get better.

**The instruction makes no clear difference.** Semantic's top 8 is the same size with and without it (23 of 27), and so is hybrid's (14 of 27), though not with the same questions. The top 50 differs by one question in its favour, and semantic's MRR by 0.02 against it.

**The cost is high once and low after that.** Embedding all the chunks takes about 2.5 hours on this laptop instead of 5 minutes, about 30 times slower, and the chunks added in Phase 5 would need the same treatment. A search takes about 0.15 s instead of 0.05 s, which nobody would notice next to the time the model takes to write.

**Decision: the final baseline stays on bge-m3.** That's what the test run used, and this experiment gives no reason to redo it. The qwen3 vectors stay in their own table, so this comparison can be repeated in seconds once the fusion is fixed in Phase 3. If semantic's lead holds up there, switching models becomes a real choice, and that one is for the project owner to make.

## The five most instructive failures

All five come from the dev baseline, so the test split stays unread. The question behind each label: was the answer in the chunks the model received? If not, it's a search problem. If yes, a reading problem.

### 1. `vocab-06`: The right chunk was first in one list and still never reached the model

**Search problem.** Πόσες ώρες την ημέρα μπορεί να δουλεύει ένα γκαρσόνι σε ταβέρνα;

Expected: Το πολύ 9 ώρες την ημέρα; Επιτρέπεται μία διακοπή έως 6 ώρες.

The model wrote: «Δεν βρέθηκε απάντηση στα διαθέσιμα κείμενα για τα γκαρσόνι σε ταβέρνα…»

Semantic search ranked the right chunk first. Keyword search didn't find it at all, because the decision says «περιποιητάς (σερβιτόρους)» and never "γκαρσόνι". Hybrid search rewards chunks that both lists found, so this one fell to rank 14, and only the first few reach the model. The model didn't have the answer and said so. That was honest, but it counts as a wrong refusal, because the answer is in our data. Fusion pushed the right chunk out of the top 8 like this for 5 of the 27 dev questions with a gold chunk (`reference-01`, `single-14`, `single-17`, `vocab-02`, `vocab-06`), although semantic search had it in its top 8. For 3 more (`reference-02`, `sector-05`, `single-21`) it stayed in hybrid's top 8 but was removed as a repeat or by the limit. This is the biggest single search finding: fusion, as built, punishes exactly the vocabulary mismatches that semantic search handles well.

Numbers: hybrid rank 14, semantic rank 1, keyword rank not found; right chunk among the 8: no; citation check: passed.

### 2. `vocab-01`: A confident answer from a decree that excludes the case, and the check passed

**Search problem.** Ποιος νόμος βάζει κανόνες ασφαλείας στις οικοδομές;

Expected: Π.Δ. 305/1996; Αφορά τα προσωρινά ή κινητά εργοτάξια; Στα αποσπάσματα υπάρχει μόνο ο τίτλος, όχι οι κανόνες.

The model wrote: «Τα κτίρια που στεγάζουν χώρους εργασίας πρέπει να έχουν δομή…»

The right document, Π.Δ. 305/1996, says "εργοτάξια" where the question says "οικοδομές". It didn't make the top 50. The model answered from Π.Δ. 16/1996 instead, whose own article 1 excludes «προσωρινά η κινητά εργοτάξια». Its quote really is in that chunk, so the citation check passed. The check proves that the words exist, not that the law applies. Finding the right document wouldn't be enough either: our copy is only its 32-word title, so the best possible answer names the decree and says its rules are missing. It's on the fetch list in [missing-laws.md](missing-laws.md).

Numbers: hybrid rank not found, semantic rank 27, keyword rank not found; right chunk among the 8: no; citation check: passed.

### 3. `single-21`: Our own limit threw away the answer, and the model filled the gap with a made-up number

**Search problem.** Πόσα παίρνω δώρο Πάσχα αν πληρώνομαι με μισθό και πόσα αν είμαι με μεροκάματο;

Expected: Μισός μηνιαίος μισθός για όσους παίρνουν μισθό; 15 ημερομίσθια για όσους παίρνουν ημερομίσθιο; Περίοδος υπολογισμού από 1 Ιανουαρίου έως 30 Απριλίου.

The model wrote: «το κατώτατο μηνιαίο μισθό του 2024…»

Search did its job: the right chunk was rank 3. But two chunks from the same decision ranked above it, and the limit of 2 per document removed it. The model saw only the sentence on the ceiling, so it gave one salary where the rule says half. Worse, it invented a minimum wage for 2024 in euros, from sources that stop in 1999, and nothing in the citation check can catch that, because it isn't a quote. With the limit at 4 the chunk arrives and the same model gives the right amounts.

Numbers: hybrid rank 3, semantic rank 2, keyword rank 14; right chunk among the 8: no; citation check: failed.

### 4. `sector-01`: The right chunk came first, and the model read the wrong line

**Reading problem.** Είμαι εργάτης σε εργοστάσιο εδώ και έξι χρόνια. Πόσες μέρες πριν πρέπει να με ειδοποιήσουν για απόλυση;

Expected: Τουλάχιστον 30 ημέρες πριν; Ισχύει για εργάτες και τεχνίτες με πάνω από πέντε χρόνια.

The model wrote: «η καταγγελία γίνεται 15 ημέρας προ της λύσεως της συμβάσεως…»

The model avoided the trap: it used the decree for workers, not the 4 months that Ν. 2112/1920 gives employees. But the article is a ladder of periods (5, 8, 15, 30 and 60 days), and for six years' service it picked the line for two years: 15 days instead of 30. It also said the decree had been repealed, misreading a note that concerns only two other clauses. Its altered quote was 90% similar to the text and the check caught it. Better search can't help here.

Numbers: hybrid rank 1, semantic rank 3, keyword rank 8; right chunk among the 8: yes; citation check: failed.

### 5. `reference-01`: A question our data can't answer got a confident wrong answer

**Reading problem.** Δουλεύω σε εργοστάσιο και κάνω υπερωρίες. Με τι ποσοστό αυξάνεται το ωρομίσθιό μου;

Expected: Το γενικό ποσοστό βρίσκεται στο άρθρο 1 του Ν. 435/1976; Το κείμενο του Ν. 435/1976 δεν υπάρχει στα αποσπάσματα; Δεν παρουσιάζει τα ποσοστά των ΚΤΕΛ ή των αρτοποιείων ως γενικό κανόνα.

The model wrote: «κάθε ώρα υπερωρίας αμειβόταν με **25% προσαύξηση**…»

The general overtime rate is in Ν. 435/1976, which isn't in our data, so the right answer is "Δεν βρέθηκε" plus a pointer to that law. The model answered 25% instead. Its source, Κ.Υ.Α. 18160/1983, is about the 41st hour of work, for workers whose pay is set by ministerial decision; the model widened it to every hour of overtime. The chunks pointing to Ν. 435/1976 weren't among the eight either (the best was first in semantic search and rank 15 after fusion). But search alone doesn't explain it: the prompt says to refuse when the chunks don't answer, and the model didn't.

Numbers: hybrid rank 15, semantic rank 1, keyword rank not found; right chunk among the 8: no; citation check: failed.

## What this means for Phase 3

In the order I'd try them, cheapest and best-measured first:

1. **Fix the fusion.** Try a lower weight for keyword search, or semantic first with keyword only as a tie-breaker. A retrieval-only run takes seconds, so this can be tested before anything else. Done in Phase 3A: see [retrieval-report.md](retrieval-report.md).
2. **Rewriter.** Vocabulary mismatches are the clearest search failure. The rewriter's job is to add the law's words: "γκαρσόνι" to "περιποιητής", "ένσημα" to "ημέρες εργασίας", "οικοδομές" to "εργοτάξια".
3. **Judge.** For reading problems the model needs to ask whether a chunk applies to this person and this case, and to refuse if none does. `sector-01` and `reference-01` both show what it has to catch. This is where limit 4 can be tried again.
4. **A stronger writer.** Some failures remain even with the right chunk and nothing confusing around it, and answers change with small changes in context. Phase 4 can show how much of this belongs to this size of model. Today's numbers can't.
5. **Fetch the missing laws.** No search or judge can answer from Ν. 435/1976 while it isn't in the data. The list is in [missing-laws.md](missing-laws.md), and 274 of those laws are already in other volumes of this dataset.
