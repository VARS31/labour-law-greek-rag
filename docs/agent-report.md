# Agent report: the agent loop with LangGraph (Phases 3C and 3C.1)

Phase 3C joins the pieces into an agent: search, a Judge that decides what can be used, more searching if it asks, then the writer. This report measures it with full answers on the dev questions, against the one-shot from Phase 3A (qwen3, semantic search, limit 2, with the repeat filter from Phase 3B). Phase 3C.1 then made two fixes, which come first because that's the agent as it is now. This file is generated by [`scripts/agent_report.py`](../scripts/agent_report.py) from the stored runs in [`eval/results/`](../eval/results/). Don't edit it by hand.

## Phase 3C.1: the two fixes

After Phase 3C, two fixes went in. **(a)** Searching stops once the Judge has approved a chunk that answers directly and applies to this person. **(b)** Round 2 also reads the next 8 places on the plain question's list. The dev questions ran once more with both, and this is the agent that goes to Phase 4, frozen. [How a question is answered](how-a-question-is-answered.md) describes it step by step and traces `single-09`.

**A small gain, and the first real rescue.** Round 2 found the right chunk for `single-09`, at rank 15, and the writer gave the main rule where 3C refused after almost 6 minutes. Wrong refusals fall from 2 to 1, key facts rise from 76% to 78%, and no question lost a key fact. But no new answer is complete, by matching or by hand. And it's one question of 27: it shows the fix can work, not how often it will.

**The stop rule believes the Judge.** It fired 5 times. On `single-09` and `single-27` the chunk really answers. On the other 3 the Judge called the wrong chunk relevant, as the table shows. It cost no key fact here. But on `refuse-06` only the writer's own refusal stood between a question outside labour law and an answer, where 3C's Judge refused in round 3. **It isn't measurably faster.** The Judge runs 40 rounds instead of 45 and uses 10% fewer tokens, but the run took 94 minutes against 91. Stopping saves rounds, and further reading makes round 2 bigger: 4 chunks more on each of the 6 questions that still reach it. The clock can't settle it either. The 23 questions that did exactly the same work took 53 and 51 minutes in all, so the machine's speed was the same overall, but one by one they took from 0.5 to 2.1 times as long in the second run, depending on when they ran. So don't compare one question's time between the two runs, and that includes the medians above.

## The result of Phase 3C

**The agent gives better answers, and the Judge's filter does that, not the extra rounds.** Checked by hand, the agent gave a complete answer to 13 of 26 answerable dev questions, against 9 for the one-shot. Key facts rise from 68% to 76%. It refused 5 of the 6 questions it should refuse, against 4, but it also wrongly refused 2 it could have answered, against 0. It is much slower: a median of 167 s per question against 63 s.

**Round 2 rescued no answer on these questions.** The right chunk reached the Judge in round 1 for 23 of 27 questions, and never for the other 4. 9 questions used more than one round: 1 got better, 2 got worse and 6 changed nothing. Those extra rounds took 22 of the 91 minutes. So the example of a rescue that the brief asks for doesn't exist here, and the report says so below.

**The gain comes from showing the writer less.** The Judge keeps about a third of what round 1 finds (84 of 256 chunks), so the writer reads 3.4 chunks on average instead of 7.9. It rejects rules for other groups, and it prefers the newest act: on `single-15` it dropped the 14 and 15 week rules and kept the 16 week one, which Phase 3B had left for someone to decide.

**Don't read too much into the size of this.** The agent found more key facts on 8 questions and fewer on 3, a split that chance alone would give about 23% of the time. This run also can't fully separate the Judge's filter from limit 4, or from how much the writer reacts to small changes in what it reads. I changed the Judge's prompt once, after trying it on 5 of these questions, and the test questions weren't touched.

## The five most instructive failures

### 1. `sector-01`: The right chunk, and the score didn't see the mistake

**Writer problem.** Είμαι εργάτης σε εργοστάσιο εδώ και έξι χρόνια. Πόσες μέρες πριν πρέπει να με ειδοποιήσουν για απόλυση;

The Judge did everything right: it rejected the rule for private employees and approved the one right chunk. The writer then told a worker with six years' service that the notice is 5 days, and that their contract lasted "between one and two years". The right answer is 30 days. It counts as complete because "30" appears further down, in the list of cases. This is why the numbers above were checked by hand. No search or Judge can fix this one.

### 2. `vocab-03`: It rejected the right chunk it had approved one round earlier

**Judge problem.** Επιτρέπεται στους φούρνους να ζυμώνουν μέσα στη νύχτα;

The chunk says «Απαγορεύεται πάσα εργασία προς παρασκευήν, ζύμωσιν και έψησιν άρτου», and the Judge's reason in round 2 is that it speaks of night work in general, "not of kneading". The writer then answered from an article about the weekend ban. The one-shot, with no Judge, got the hours right.

### 3. `reference-01`: It rejected five rules for other groups, then fell for the sixth

**Judge problem.** Δουλεύω σε εργοστάσιο και κάνω υπερωρίες. Με τι ποσοστό αυξάνεται το ωρομίσθιό μου;

In round 1 it rejected the rules for textile mills, furniture makers, KTEL, kitchens and cinemas, which the one-shot can't do. But it kept a 1979 decision that pays 25% for hours beyond the agreed week, and the general rate is in Ν. 435/1976, which isn't in our data. The chunks pointing to that law rank too low to reach the Judge. So the answer is the same wrong 25% as the one-shot's.

### 4. `vocab-01`: The word never comes up, so the Judge chooses among wrong chunks

**Search problem.** Ποιος νόμος βάζει κανόνες ασφαλείας στις οικοδομές;

The right document says "εργοτάξια" and the question says "οικοδομές". Plain search misses it, and the Query Enhancer never produces that word. The Judge can only choose from what it gets, and it approved 7 chunks, mostly from a decree about ordinary workplaces. Nothing warned the reader.

### 5. `single-09`: Almost six minutes, then an honest refusal that counts as wrong

**Search problem.** Ο εργοδότης μού έκοψε το επίδομα χωρίς να με ρωτήσει. Τι σημαίνει αυτό για τη σύμβασή μου, αφού είμαι υπάλληλος;

After three rounds the Judge refused, and the refusal points to Ν. 2112/1920, the right law. The one-shot wrote a wrong answer instead. The score calls the refusal wrong, but I'd rather have it.

## How the agent works

This is the frozen agent, after Phase 3C.1. [How a question is answered](how-a-question-is-answered.md) gives every rule in full.

```
question
   |
   v
search: the plain question, qwen3, limit 4, keyword places if it names a law or article
   |
   v
Judge: one verdict per chunk, then answer, search again or refuse <-----+
   |                  |                                                 |
   |                  '--> search again (at most 3 rounds in all) ------+
   |                       not if an approved chunk answers directly: the code writes instead
   |                       round 2: the next 8 places on the plain list, the enhancer's phrasings,
   |                                laws the Judge named, articles next to approved chunks
   |                       round 3: what the Judge said was missing and the same phrasings,
   |                                laws the Judge named, articles next to approved chunks
   |
   +--> write: approved chunks + the person's own question, then the citation check
   '--> refuse: written by the code, names where the rule would be when known
```

The numbers in this section are from the Phase 3C run.

- **The Judge** is one call to `ministral-3:8b` at temperature 0, with its instructions in [`prompts/judge.md`](../prompts/judge.md). For each chunk it gives relevant, partly or not relevant; whether it applies to this person; which group it's only for; and whether a newer act replaces it. A chunk is approved if it's relevant or partly, doesn't fail to apply, and isn't replaced.
- **The code checks the Judge,** because a JSON schema fixes only the shape. A "newer" act must really be newer. A law it names must be cited in the chunks: the code dropped 31 that weren't, mostly the chunk's own act. The last round can't search again.
- **A law that isn't in our data is mentioned** in the answer or the refusal, by the code. All 11 such claims in this run are true: I checked them against [missing-laws.md](missing-laws.md) and the chunks.
- **The prompt's example is on a topic outside the test set,** and `tests/test_agent.py` checks that.
- **It stays local.** LangGraph brings LangSmith, LangChain's tracing service, with it. It is switched off in the code. One question run with all outside connections blocked tried none.
- **Each round is recorded** in the result: chunks, verdicts, reasons and time.

## What I'd try next

Phase 3C listed four things to try. Phase 3C.1 did the first two, and the agent is now frozen, so the other two would be a new system with its own run, after Phase 4.

1. **Done: stop when a right chunk is in hand.** It saves a round when the Judge is right, on `single-27`. It keeps the right chunk in front of the writer on `vocab-03`, but the answer is no better.
2. **Done: let round 2 read further down the plain question's list.** It got the one rescue, `single-09`. `vocab-01` stops before reaching it, and its right chunk is too far down in any case.
3. **A stronger writer.** 11 of the 14 failures in 3C.1 are the writer's, with the right chunk in front of it. Phase 4 can test that.
4. **A faster, more careful Judge.** It takes 73% of the agent's time, mostly writing one verdict for each chunk. Now that its "relevant" can end the search, its mistakes also cost more.

## What this doesn't show

- **27 questions, read closely.** One question is about 4 points. I read these answers, and I changed the Judge's prompt once after trying 5 of them, so the numbers flatter the agent. The test questions weren't touched, and the agent's one test run belongs to Phase 4.
- **One run of a model that reacts to small changes.** The Judge runs at temperature 0, but a small change in the chunks can change its verdicts, as `vocab-03` shows.
- **The time depends on the machine.** The one-shot's median here is 63 s, and it was 36 s in Phase 2 on a quieter machine. The Mac also slept during part of the agent run, which the per-question times leave out. Compare the two systems roughly, not to the second.
- **A refusal's pointer can mislead.** On `refuse-06` the refusal says the chunks point to Ν.Δ. 3868/1958, a law on family allowances named in an earlier round, although the question is about custody. All 11 claims that a law isn't in our data were checked and are true, but true doesn't always mean relevant.
- **The scores can't read meaning.** Besides the wrong answers named above, `single-01` is right on the main point and scores only 1 of 3 facts. I read the answers scored as complete, not all the others.
