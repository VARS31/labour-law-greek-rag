I judged each answer against the text of its chunks only, not against current law. "Correct" means the direct answer is right and nothing important misleads. **What goes wrong**

- **The model mixes things up.** Rules for one sector or group become general rules (4, 6, 7, 8), cases get swapped (3), and details are invented (2, 7, 8). I haven't tried a larger model, so I can't say how much of this comes down to the size of this one.
- **Retrieval leaves out the answer.** The limit of 2 chunks per document removed the main rule in question 7 and maternity leave in question 8 (details in the section on repeats). In questions 3 and 6, search never found the key text.
- **A passing check doesn't mean a right answer.** It proves that quoted words exist in the cited chunk. It says nothing about the sentences around them. Questions 1, 6 and 8 pass, and question 6 is wrong. A failed check is a real warning, though: every failed quote here was altered, invented, cited to the wrong chunk, or had no citation.

**The prompt was revised once.** The first run was far from the short answers asked for, so I capped the explanation at five sentences, limited quotes to fifteen words in a row, banned made-up examples and calculations, and required sector rules to be labelled. ## How an answer is made

1. **Search:** hybrid search (semantic + keyword, Reciprocal Rank Fusion) returns its top 30 chunks.
2. **Remove repeats:** walking down that list, a chunk is skipped if at least 50% of its words are shared with a chunk already chosen, or if its document already has 2 chunks. The first 8 that pass are numbered [1] to [8].
3. **Answer:** one call to `ministral-3:8b` through Ollama, temperature 0.2, seed 42 (so the answer can be repeated), context window 16,384 tokens. The instructions are in [`prompts/answer.md`](../prompts/answer.md); the chunks follow the question, each with its source, article, title and year.
4. **Closing note:** the fixed note from `prompts/closing_note.md` must end the answer; the code adds it if the model forgot.
5. **Citation check** (`labour_rag/citations.py`): every [n] must point to one of the chunks given; every quote in «…» must carry a citation and its words must appear in that chunk (accents, case and punctuation ignored; "…" may skip words). An answer that isn't a refusal must cite something. Warnings, which don't fail the check: sentences without a citation, no year named, closing note missing. When a quote fails, the most similar passage of the chunk is shown with its similarity, to tell a quote with small changes from one that isn't there.
