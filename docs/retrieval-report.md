# Retrieval report: fixing the fusion step (Phase 3A)

Phase 2 found that search often finds the right chunk and then loses it when the semantic and keyword lists are combined. This phase tries five ways of combining them. Retrieval only, on the dev questions: no writer, no prompt changes. This file is generated by [`scripts/retrieval_report.py`](../scripts/retrieval_report.py) from the stored runs in [`eval/results/`](../eval/results/). Don't edit it by hand.

Phase 3B has since made qwen3 the default, on the project owner's decision, and fixed the repeat filter described below: see [enhancer-report.md](enhancer-report.md). The numbers here are from before those changes.

## The result

**Winner: (c), semantic search alone.** With bge-m3 and limit 2, the right chunk now reaches the writer for **17 of 27** dev questions, up from 13 of 27. (d) and (e) reach the same number, and the rule says that within one question the simpler way wins. Semantic search alone is the new default. The old way stays selectable with `--fusion rrf`, in `labour_rag.ask` and `labour_rag.evaluate`.

**Why the old way lost.** Our RRF uses the constant 60 and takes 50 chunks from each list. So a chunk found by both lists scores at least 2/110 = 0.0182, and the first chunk of one list scores 1/61 = 0.0164. Any chunk in both lists, even 50th in each, beat the first result of either list. Keyword search accepts any word of the question, so it finds a lot, and that pushed down what semantic search had ranked high. Doubling semantic's weight (b) helps but doesn't cure it: below semantic's rank 13, a chunk can still lose to one that is 50th in both lists.

## What moved

Compared with the old way, on bge-m3 and limit 2, the right chunk now arrives for 6 more questions (`reference-01`, `reference-02`, `single-14`, `single-21`, `vocab-02`, `vocab-06`) and no longer arrives for 2 (`sector-02`, `single-15`).

**A problem this change exposes: `single-15`.** The right chunk (Π.Δ. 176/1997, 16 weeks of maternity leave) is 2nd in semantic search, behind an older decision (Υ.Α. 12756/1989, 15 weeks). Their words overlap by 60%, and the repeat filter ignores numbers, so it calls them repeats and keeps whichever comes first. Under the old fusion the newer one came first. Now the writer gets 15 weeks, where the expected answer is 16. So the filter can remove a newer law because it looks like the older one. I didn't change the filter, because this phase is about fusion, but it should be fixed before the agent relies on it.

**The 10 questions that still miss.** The per-document limit removes 4, the repeat filter removes 1, and 5 rank too low. The rewriter in Phase 3 is aimed at that last group.

## Does qwen3 beat bge-m3?

**Yes. With the winning fusion, qwen3 beats bge-m3 by 5 of 27 questions at limit 2 (22 against 17) and by 4 at limit 4 (23 against 19). That clears the bar of 3.** The default stays bge-m3: this choice is the project owner's.

- **Which questions, at limit 2:** it gains `sector-02`, `single-10`, `single-15`, `single-17`, `single-29`, `vocab-03` and loses `reference-01`.
- **How sure:** a split of 6 to 1 would happen by chance about 12% of the time. That's suggestive, on a small sample.
- **Without the query instruction:** 21 at limit 2 and 23 at limit 4, so the result doesn't depend on it.
- **Cost:** the embedding is already done, and took about 2.5 hours. A search takes about 0.12 s instead of 0.03 s. New chunks in Phase 5 would be embedded at the same slow speed, about 30 times slower than bge-m3.
- **Not measured:** whether the writer gives better answers with it.

## Refusal detection

An answer now counts as a refusal if it says near its start that the answer isn't in the sources, in the prompt's words ("Δεν βρέθηκε") or in others ("δεν αναφέρεται στα αποσπάσματα", "τα αποσπάσματα δεν αναφέρουν"). A sentence that goes on with "αλλά" doesn't count, because it gives an answer after all, and neither does a missing detail later in an answer. The rule was written from dev answers only. All stored answers were scored again.

| Run | Answers that changed | Correct refusals | Wrong refusals |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Dev, limit 2 | 1 | 4 → 4 of 6 | 1 → 2 of 26 |
| Dev, limit 4 | 0 | 4 → 4 of 6 | 1 → 1 of 26 |
| Test, limit 2 | 1 | 2 → 2 of 4 | 1 → 2 of 16 |

| Run | Question | Should it be refused? | Now counts as | Citation check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dev, limit 2 | `lookup-01` | no | wrong refusal | failed, now passes |
| Test, limit 2 | `lookup-02` | no | wrong refusal | same |

`lookup-01` wrote «Το άρθρο 2 του Ν. 1837/1989 δεν αναφέρεται στα αποσπάσματα». It had no citation, so it used to fail the citation check as an answer that cites nothing. That's why the check on dev goes from 10 to 11 of 26. `lookup-02` is a test question, so I didn't read its answer. Its numbers fit a refusal (0 of 2 key facts, right chunk not among the 8), but only a person reading it can confirm that. Nothing else in the past runs changes, and the eight Phase 1C answers don't either. [`docs/eval-report.md`](eval-report.md) now shows these numbers.

## What this doesn't show

- **No answers were written.** More right chunks may not mean better answers: in Phase 2, limit 4 brought 3 more right chunks and no better answers. Only a run with the writer can show this.
- **The winner is flattered.** It was chosen on the same 27 questions it's measured on, and one question is about 4 points. The test questions weren't touched.
- **The baseline on test is still the old setup.** `2026-11-04_oneshot-limit2-test` used equal-weight RRF, and Phase 4 compares against those numbers. The new default has no test number, because the one-shot system has used its one run.
- **Refusals weren't measured.** Questions that must be refused have no gold chunk, so they don't count in any number above.
